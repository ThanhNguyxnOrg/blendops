# Wide-shot camera rules

## Lens choice

| Use case | Equivalent focal length |
|---|---|
| Cinematic ultrawide vista | 14–20mm |
| Standard wide establishing | 24–35mm |
| Compressed cityscape | 50–85mm (compresses depth) |
| Telephoto distant landscape | 100mm+ (very compressed) |

## Altitude

| Altitude | Effect |
|---|---|
| Ground / eye-level (1.6–1.8m) | Human perspective; relatable |
| Slightly elevated (3–10m) | Hero shot of subject in environment |
| Drone height (20–100m) | Aerial overview; reduces human scale |
| Top-down / orthographic | Map-like; abstract |

## Tilt

| Tilt | Effect |
|---|---|
| Level (no tilt) | Stable, neutral |
| Slight up (Dutch +5°) | Heroic, looking up |
| Slight down (Dutch −5°) | Authoritative, surveying |
| Strong tilt (>15°) | Disorienting; use rarely |

## Common wide-shot positions

- **Hero approach**: ground level, slight up tilt, subject silhouette dominates frame.
- **Establishing aerial**: 30m elevation, level, full landscape visible.
- **Worm's eye**: ground level, strong up tilt, subject looms.
- **Top-down**: orthographic, map view, abstract.

## Avoid

- Strong fisheye distortion on architectural establishing shots.
- Eye-level wide angle on small subject (subject reads as tiny dot).
- Extreme tilt without narrative justification.

## Aspect-specific notes

- 16:9 → standard establishing
- 2.39:1 → exaggerate horizontal scope; compress vertical
- 9:16 → portrait orientation; elevate subject placement; minimize sky if no clouds
- 1:1 → minimal negative space; tight crop required
