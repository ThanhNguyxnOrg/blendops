# Composition rules (rule of thirds, subject placement, hierarchy)

These are heuristics, not laws. Apply per framing intent.

## Rule of thirds

Divide the frame into 9 equal parts (3 × 3). Place primary subjects on the lines or intersection points (not center) for editorial / cinematic intent.

| Framing | Subject position |
|---|---|
| Editorial portrait | Eye line on upper-third horizontal |
| Hero card with CTA | Subject on right-third (or left-third) vertical line |
| Cinematic landscape | Horizon on upper or lower third |
| Symmetric brand hero | Center is OK; rule of thirds optional |

## Subject placement

| Intent | Placement |
|---|---|
| Symmetric brand hero | Centered |
| Editorial / dynamic | Off-center on third |
| Catalog product (with negative space for text) | Right or left third |
| Documentary (off-camera focus) | Far edge with negative space toward viewer's gaze |

## Visual hierarchy

The eye reads:
1. Brightest spot first
2. Highest contrast next
3. Sharpest focus next
4. Largest object next
5. Most saturated color next

Hierarchy verdicts:
- Pass: subject claims at least 3 of these.
- Warn: subject claims 1–2.
- Fail: subject claims 0 (eye reads a non-subject as primary).

## Negative space

Negative space is **intentional**:
- "Soft hero" → wide negative space allowed for headline.
- "Editorial" → negative space carries mood.
- "Catalog" → negative space minimal; tight crop.

Verdict: match against intent. Wide negative space without brief justification → Warn or Fail.

## Depth cues

- Atmospheric perspective (haze) for outdoor depth.
- Overlap (foreground occluding mid/background).
- Size diminishment.
- Bokeh / depth of field.

A 3D scene without depth cues reads as flat. Hero close-up may intentionally minimize depth; verify intent.
