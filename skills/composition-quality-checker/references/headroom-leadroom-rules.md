# Headroom + lead room rules

## Headroom (space above subject)

| Subject | Recommended headroom (% of frame height) |
|---|---|
| Tight portrait close-up | 5–10% |
| Medium portrait shot | 10–18% |
| Wide / full body | 15–25% |
| Hero product (architectural) | 10–20% |

### Verdicts

- Pass: within recommended range.
- Warn: 0% (head touching top is uncomfortable for most uses) OR > 35% (excessive).
- Fail: head cropped (subject head extends above frame) when not stylistically justified.

## Lead room (space in direction of motion or gaze)

| Subject | Lead room |
|---|---|
| Subject looking left | More space on left of subject |
| Subject moving right | More space on right of subject |
| Static centered subject | Lead room not applicable |

### Verdicts

- Pass: lead room aligns with motion / gaze direction.
- Warn: lead room slightly off (within 10% of frame width).
- Fail: subject "pressed against" frame edge in motion / gaze direction → reads as trapped.

## Common mistakes

- Placing the subject's eyes at the dead vertical center (instead of upper third).
- Excessive headroom for tight close-ups.
- Lead room behind the subject (anti-motion direction).
- Treating "centered" as universally safe (it is not for editorial intent).

## Per-aspect adjustments

- 1:1 (square) → headroom can be slightly less; subject often centered.
- 9:16 (portrait mobile) → headroom and chinroom equally important; subject typically upper third.
- 2.39:1 (cinematic) → exaggerate horizontal lead room; verticals less critical.
